I'm planning the program for a church mission trip to Ethiopia. The theme is "On the Rock", from the parable of the wise and foolish builders in Matthew 7:24–27. It runs for ten days. There is a Vacation Bible School for kids during the day and a youth program in the evening.

## Status

The concept and the logo are done, and I'm working through the plan one day at a time. Day 1 is planned and has a short printable version. The rest of the days are still to come.

## Decisions

- The youth evenings are for both the local youth and the young people on our own team.
- We expect 100 to 200 kids, outdoors. Each day starts with everyone together: songs, a skit of the story where the kids play the storm, the verse, and a "Rock vs Sand" game. Then the kids split into age groups (4–6, 7–9 and 10–12) of about 22 each, with an adult lead and a youth helper, for snack and craft. At the end everyone comes back together to close.
- I adopted two traditions. Ebenezer stones are small memorial stones, after 1 Samuel 7:12. Palanca letters are personal letters of encouragement. The stones don't go home with the kids. They are laid together at the house we share, so they build up over the ten days.
- The Day 1 line is "Build on the Rock!" I rejected "Listen well. Do good. Stand strong."
- Not every child comes from a church background, so the wording stays gentle and welcoming.
- Planning happens in plain documents, one per day, with a section each for kids, youth and preparation. I first built a custom planner website for it and dropped it. It put too much on one page. What I wanted was a real document with the details of one day, not an overview of everything.
- Each day's printout is short: two or three pages with the run sheet, the story and the craft. My first version of Day 1 was ten pages, which was far too much.
- The final printouts come in English and Russian. I'll translate and compile them once, when the whole plan is finished, not day by day.
